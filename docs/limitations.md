# Limitations and claim boundaries

## What this project establishes

- A reproducible extraction of a 12-surface pilot from a pinned EVA source.
- Observable line, position, repetition, and token-frequency features.
- A traceable creative English layer whose generation rules are public.
- A publication format that keeps analysis and fiction apart.

## What this project does not establish

- The language, cipher, script type, author, or intended reader of the manuscript.
- Phonetic values for Voynich glyphs.
- English meanings for EVA tokens.
- Secure identifications of illustrated plants, stars, bodies, containers, or activities.
- That the manuscript is encrypted natural language, invented language, shorthand, a generative system, or meaningless text.
- A full-manuscript translation.

## Why the English version is not a translation

The English wording is selected from section-specific vocabularies informed by the illustrations and conventional scholarly section names. It is not derived by substituting, decoding, or linguistically analyzing Voynich tokens. A different vocabulary could produce a different but equally fluent story from the same page. That underdetermination is decisive.

## Source limitations

EVA is a transliteration convention, not the manuscript itself. Transcriber judgments, uncertain glyphs, layout encodings, ligatures, damaged surfaces, and line ordering can all affect counts. This pilot uses one current composite transcription and preserves its raw locus text, but does not resolve disagreements among transcription systems.

The included folio images are reduced-size research references, not substitutes for Yale's high-resolution digital objects. Foldouts and circular writing are especially difficult to represent in a linear document.

## Statistical limitations

Frequency is not meaning. Repetition, word-shape families, positional restrictions, and section differences can constrain hypotheses, but they do not identify plaintext on their own. The pilot does not perform model comparison, significance testing, historical-language matching, or blinded prediction.

## AI limitations

This edition was prepared with OpenAI Codex using GPT-5.6 Sol at max reasoning effort. Language models are optimized to produce plausible continuations. Plausibility can make an unsupported reading feel persuasive. For that reason:

- every creative line is labeled;
- the analytical edition contains no generated semantic gloss;
- deterministic generation is documented;
- no “first translation,” “solved,” or confidence percentage is claimed;
- human specialist review is required before stronger claims.

## Related claims

Other public AI-assisted projects claim readings of the manuscript. Their existence means this project should not claim priority merely for using AI. No related claim is treated as validated unless its procedure generalizes, makes risky predictions, and survives independent review.
